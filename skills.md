# Infocore — Visual Skill

## When to Use
Use this aesthetic for data visualization, infographics, presentations, dashboards, or any design requiring structured communication of complex information. Ideal for contexts emphasizing clarity, authority, and accessibility.

## Aesthetic Identity
Infocore focuses on clean, organized layouts with high contrast and visual clarity. It balances academic and utilitarian tones through restrained typography paired with bold graphic elements. Vibrant color accents emphasize salient data points and guide attention. The aesthetic is systematic yet approachable.

## Color System
The palette uses striking primary and secondary colors—such as reds, blues, yellows, and teals—paired with muted neutrals like grays, tans, and whites. Saturation is high for accents and gentle for backgrounds. Avoid muddy or pastel tones. Vibrancy is essential for overlay graphics and icons.

## Composition Rules
Designs rely on grids, modular sections, and balanced alignment strategies. Whitespace is limited but consciously utilized to separate sections. Symmetry, proportionality, and hierarchy guide layouts. Focal points exist through larger text or vibrant shapes. Overcrowding is avoided with clear data segmentation.

## Typography
Sans-serif fonts dominate, with variations in weights to create hierarchy. Font sizes escalate for headings, followed by subheadings and body text. Tracking is tight, and text is primarily left-aligned. Use consistent case treatments, favoring title case for headlines and standard sentence case for body text.

## Material & Surface
The aesthetic evokes smooth, digital surfaces. Gradients and subtle shadows enhance visuals without overwhelming them. Line graphs, pie charts, and bar charts feature clean lines, geometric precision, and vector-quality finishes. Texture use is minimal, commonly smooth or flat.

## Cultural Anchors
Infocore descends from late 20th and early 21st-century infographic design trends, influenced by digital journalism and corporate presentations. It borrows from the Swiss Design movement’s clarity and preferring geometry, grids, and objectivity. It avoids clutter and overly playful representations.

## Exclusions
- Hand-drawn illustrations or textures.
- Overly ornate or serif typography.
- Dark, moody, or muted palettes.
- Overwhelming complexity or illegible overcrowding.
- Irregular, unstructured layouts.

## Generative Instructions
1. Set up a grid-based layout with defined modular sections for content placement.
2. Select a high-contrast color palette featuring primary colors (red, blue, yellow) and neutrals like white or gray. Use vibrant accents for highlights.
3. Choose sans-serif typefaces with consistent weights. Use bold for headers and lighter for body text. Apply clear text hierarchy (title > subtitle > body).
4. Enforce alignment within each section and between sections. Use wide margins to prevent dense overcrowding.
5. Include clean vector-based graphics and charts (e.g., bar graphs, pie charts) with strong, defined edges.
6. Avoid using textures or shadows excessively, keeping surfaces smooth and polished.
7. Design icons and shapes geometrically with sharp edges or rounded corners, avoiding irregular or hand-drawn designs.
8. Include spacing between content blocks to enhance readability and visual flow.
9. Use title case for headers, paired with sentence case body copy for clarity and a professional tone.
10. Test for legibility and functionality by ensuring each section communicates effectively even when viewed independently.
